# Why the homepage is built this way

## The one decision everything follows from

The old homepage sold a mechanism ("shape belief into permanance", "signal seeding", "narrative
camouflage") to buyers who shop for an outcome. It blurred its own logos and asked for the sale
with "Learn More".

The new page sells **one outcome**, *own your category*, and **one next step**: **the Category
Review**. That's a 30-minute call where a senior lead walks the capabilities deck against the
prospect's category. The offer uses the deck's own closing promise (P.54): *how we'd run it, who'd
run it, what it costs*. The page and the call make the same promise in the same words, so the call
feels like the next page of the website. The deck stops being a pitch and becomes the continuation.

The copy came from a panel process:

1. Three complete drafts, each written independently through one lens (Hormozi, Gary Vee, Chris Do).
2. Scoring by three judges: a skeptical buyer panel, a CRO expert, and a fact-checker against the deck.
3. A merge of the best sections from all three drafts.
4. An adversarial fact-check of every number against the deck.

All three judges converged on the same shape: **Chris Do's structure and tone, Hormozi's offer
mechanics inside it, and Gary's channel specifics on top.** All three picked the same hero line and
the same offer.

## What each lens contributed

### Alex Hormozi: the offer and the value equation

- **A named offer with three named parts.** The Category Review is the call. The **Visibility
  Snapshot** comes before it and the **Category Plan** comes after. The first Hormozi draft had six
  named mechanics. The judges found that hurt five-second comprehension, so it was cut to three.
- **The value equation, applied section by section:**
  - *Dream outcome:* "Own your category."
  - *Perceived likelihood:* deck-counted proof above the fold (4,125+ projects, 5.31 average ROAS,
    460+ deals) and eight case studies with the headline number over the image.
  - *Time delay:* the Snapshot lands before the call, a cost range comes *on* the call, the Plan
    follows in three business days, and work is in market in eight weeks.
  - *Effort and sacrifice:* named seniors, a three-month minimum then rolling, you own the agents,
    data and work, and you keep the Snapshot, the three moves and the Plan whether you hire PAVE or
    not.
- **The value stack sits high (right after the proof band),** so a skimmer sees what the call gets them before
  reading eight case studies.
- **A lead magnet that solves a narrow problem and exposes the next one.** The Snapshot shows
  *where* you're invisible against two competitors. Fixing it is the call.
- **A pain → fix table** in the diagnosis. Every scar the buyer carries (junior hand-off,
  impressions reporting, slow starts, lock-in) is answered with a term straight from the deck.
- **What we cut**, because it tipped into infomercial for enterprise CMOs: dollar-value stacks,
  month-named "intake" urgency, "Teardown" as the offer name (it sounds like an attack on the buyer's
  team), and a second-session guarantee (kept as an A/B test below).

### Gary Vaynerchuk: attention, channels, energy

- **"Where attention is underpriced" as a teaching section.** It covers four rooms the buyer checks:
  *the answer* (ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity/Copilot), *the thread* (Reddit/Quora/forums), *the screen*
  (Pave TV: 500+ apps, 120M+ U.S. households) and *the feed* (one asset → dozens of native cuts). Each
  room ends on an "Ask yourself" question the reader can't answer comfortably. "Underpriced" is framed
  as PAVE's view, never as "cheap": to a premium buyer, "cheap" signals a cheap-media shop.
- **Operator-level pain lines:** "You asked ChatGPT for the best option in your category. You
  weren't in the answer." "The creative worked in March. It's been dying since April."
- **Speed and volume** throughout: ten versions before lunch, eight weeks to market, weekly reporting.
- **The announcement bar**: a free, concrete, category-specific deliverable in one line. The buyer
  panel rated it the clearest hook in any draft.

### Chris Do: positioning, diagnosis, qualification, calm

- **Diagnose before prescribing.** The page runs diagnosis (why your competitor seems to be
  everywhere), then teaching (the four rooms), then prescription (one team, one plan, one number),
  then proof, then the offer.
- **Clear positioning in one sentence:** who (mid-market and enterprise brands), what (one senior
  team for media, creative and AI), outcome (show up everywhere buyers check).
- **A peer-level call.** A minute-by-minute agenda (0–5 / 5–20 / 20–30) and *a realistic cost range
  on the call, not "we'll follow up with a proposal."* The buyer panel picked this as the line most
  likely to make a founder book.
- **Qualification in the open.** "For you if / not for you if" lists sit next to the calendar. The
  form asks for budget, role and timeline. Premium buyers read that as confidence, and it keeps
  tire-kickers off the calendar.
- **Calm tone.** No countdowns and no begging. The only scarcity line gives a reason: *the people on
  the call run the work, so we take on a limited number of new engagements at a time.*

### The conversion path

- **Primary:** any CTA → the calendar card (`#book-cal`) → Calendly slot → booking questions → Snapshot emailed the day
  before → 30-minute Category Review → Category Plan within three business days → three-month
  engagement.
- **Secondary:** hero / Snapshot band → `#snapshot` form in the footer card → Snapshot within two
  business days → one follow-up offering a time. After submitting, the success state goes straight
  to a pre-filled Calendly with the visitor's name, email and category, so hot leads can book right
  away.
- **Consistency rules:** one primary CTA label everywhere ("Book my Category Review"). Every
  in-section pill goes to the calendar card. Turnaround times and Snapshot scope are the same wherever they
  appear.

### Old page vs. new page

| Old homepage | New homepage |
|---|---|
| A vague mechanism ("shape belief into permanance", with a typo) | Outcome and positioning in one sentence |
| Blurred logos: "brands that don't want you to know" | Proof carried by numbers and eight case studies; logos and client names saved for the deck walkthrough |
| No numbers | 4,125+ / 5.31 / 460+ above the fold, plus eight case studies with charts |
| "Learn More" / "Drop us a line" | A named offer with a stacked value proposition, a pre-call deliverable, a cost range on the call and a written plan |
| No qualification | Role, category, budget and timeline in the form, plus for / not-for lists by the calendar |
| Nothing connects the site to the sales process | The page previews the deck in the deck's own order, so the call continues the same story |

## A/B test backlog (highest leverage first)

1. **Hero line.** "Own your category." (control) vs. "Invisible to inevitable." with the VP of
   Marketing quote as attribution. *Hypothesis:* a line borrowed from a client reads as proof, not a
   slogan, and lifts hero → booking clicks among burned CMOs. *Metric:* bookings per unique visitor.
2. **Guarantee line.** Add "Leave without three moves you'd actually run and we'll book a second
   session with a channel lead, free." *Hypothesis:* explicit risk reversal lifts founder and
   mid-market bookings without lowering enterprise quality. *Guardrail:* share of bookings in the
   $100K+ budget bands.
3. **Published fee floor.** Add "Engagements typically start at $X/month in fees, plus media" to the
   pricing FAQ. *Hypothesis:* fewer total bookings but a higher qualified share and close rate, so
   less wasted senior time. *Metric:* close rate per 100 bookings.
4. **Offer placement.** The full offer block after the proof band (control) vs. a two-line teaser there with
   the full stack only next to the calendar. *Metric:* bookings per session, split by device.
5. **Booking flow.** The inline Calendly with required questions (control) vs. a three-field
   qualifier (category, budget, role) first, then the calendar. *Hypothesis:* easy questions first
   lift completion through commitment and consistency. *Metric:* embed-start → confirmed booking.
6. **Proof row slot 3.** "8 wks to market" (control) vs. "−41% cost per qualified lead" tagged *one
   client*. Segment by the role and category answers. *Hypothesis:* the B2B stat lifts B2B visitors
   and the speed stat lifts DTC/CPG visitors.
7. **Form length.** Eight required fields (control) vs. five (name, email, website, category,
   budget), with role and timeline asked on the success screen. *Hypothesis:* a 25%+ lift in
   submissions without losing qualification data.
8. **Secondary hero CTA.** "Get my free Visibility Snapshot" (control) vs. "See the numbers ↓".
   *Metric:* total conversions per visitor, with primary bookings as the guardrail.

All CTA clicks, form starts and submits, service opens, results filters, FAQ opens, calendar embeds
and Calendly bookings are pushed to `window.dataLayer`. Each test above can be read in GA4 once GTM
is on the page.
