# The Category Review: 30-minute call playbook

**The job of the call:** pay off every promise the homepage made, in order, and end with a clear
yes, no or next step. The prospect should recognize the deck from the page they just read. The page
previews it section by section, in the same pillar order.

Deck page numbers below refer to *Pave Capabilities 2026 v3* (54 pages).

---

## Before the call (T-2 to T-1 business days)

1. **Read the booking answers:** the category sentence, two competitors, website and budget band.
   Configure these four as required questions on the Calendly event (see README).
2. **Route it to the lead who would run the account** (media, growth/search, creative or AI), based
   on the category and budget. The homepage promises "the senior lead you meet runs your account."
3. **Build the Visibility Snapshot**: one page, the prospect vs. two competitors.
   - **Google:** who owns page one for 3–5 category terms.
   - **The answer:** ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot the same 3–5 buyer questions and log
     who gets named. This mirrors the P.21 model visibility audit and competitive share, and P.20
     answer monitoring.
   - **The thread:** the top 2–3 Reddit or forum threads about the category, and whether the
     prospect appears (P.29 community intelligence).
   - **Branded search page:** what a buyer sees when they search the brand (P.31 brand SERP, P.33
     "What they find when they check").
   - **One line:** the biggest gap.
   - The screen (streaming TV) and the feed (short-form, creators) are covered live on the call.
     Prepare one observation on each: are the competitors visibly running streaming TV or creator
     work?
4. **Pick the case study to show:** the one from P.42–P.49 closest to their situation (Revenue /
   Leads & B2B / Search / Culture, the same filters as the homepage).
5. **Email at T-1 business day:** the Snapshot, the three-line agenda (the same 0–5 / 5–20 / 20–30
   split as the homepage), and "If you want an NDA first, reply and we'll send one."

---

## 0:00–0:02 · Open

- "You told us you intend to own **[their category sentence]**. Is that still the right sentence?"
- "What's the one number you're judged on?" (If they filled in "the one number you need to move" on
  the form, confirm it.)
- Set the frame: "Snapshot first, then our deck against your category, then who'd run it, a cost
  range and a straight answer on fit. If we're not right for you, I'll say so."

*Pays off:* "It's a working session" (FAQ) and "a senior lead, not a salesperson" (#book).

## 0:02–0:07 · The Snapshot and the four rooms

- **Snapshot, about 3 minutes:** page one, the answer, the thread, the branded search page. For each:
  who's there, where they're missing, and what closing the gap would take.
- **The other two rooms live, about 2 minutes:** **the screen** (P.09–P.10 Pave TV: 500+ environments,
  120M+ U.S. households) and **the feed** (P.12 "Ten versions before lunch", P.13 creators).
- Ask: *"Which of these surprised you?"* Their answer tells you which pillar to lead with.

*Pays off:* the announcement bar, the hero's secondary CTA, section 05 ("Your Snapshot checks the
first two… we cover the other two on the call") and the Snapshot checklist.

## 0:07–0:20 · The deck, against their category

1. **P.03 Position.** FIG. 01: "The people in this deck are the people on the account. No junior
   hand-off after signature." Say which leads you'd name in their Category Plan.
2. **P.05 Three pillars, one operating model.** "Most clients start in one pillar." It's the same
   order and numbering as the homepage services list. Name the pillar you'd start with and why.
   *Pays off:* the services list and the FAQ "Do we have to switch everything?"
3. **P.07 The funnel.** Place the Snapshot gaps on Reach / Demand / Consideration / Conversion /
   Retention. The homepage's funnel tags come from this page.
4. **Two or three capability pages** for the starting pillar:
   - Revenue and paid: P.08 paid media, P.14 creative assets, P.12 generative production
   - Reach: P.09–P.10 Pave TV
   - Search and AI answers: P.20 AEO, P.21 GEO, P.30 SEO/AEO/AI search, P.31 Instant SEO
   - B2B pipeline: P.22 marketing automation, P.25 data ("One number everyone agrees on")
   - Culture and creators: P.13 creators, P.15 music, P.41 (460+ deals, selected bookings)
   - Always-on capacity: P.17–P.18 agents ("Nothing here runs unattended"), P.19 governance
5. **The closest case study** (P.42–P.49): the challenge, what we did and the takeaway, the same way
   the homepage card tells it. *Pays off:* "Walk me through this one" and "Find the one like mine."
6. **Their first three moves** for weeks 1–8. Say them out loud and let them write them down. The
   moves are theirs either way.
7. **Second opinion.** If they brought their current agency's plan: two things you'd keep, two you'd
   change, one you'd stop.

*Pays off:* the stack items "Our playbook, against your category", "Your first three moves" and "A
second opinion".

## 0:20–0:25 · How we'd run it, who'd run it, what it costs

- **P.51 How we work:** Diagnose (weeks 1–2), Design (weeks 3–4), Deploy (weeks 5–8), Compound. "Eight
  weeks from first look to work in market."
- **P.52 Standard scope:** which rows apply, their cadence and **the named lead for each
  workstream**.
- **A cost range:** a realistic monthly fee range, kept separate from media spend. Never close with
  "we'll follow up with a proposal." The homepage promises a range on the call.
- **Terms (P.52):** three-month minimum, rolling after that. Named seniors. They own the agents, the
  data and the work. Their team approves every outward step.

## 0:25–0:30 · Fit and close

Qualifying questions (ask whichever the form and booking questions didn't answer):

1. "Who else signs off on a decision like this, and what do they need to see?"
2. "What's the monthly media budget, separate from agency fees?"
3. "If we started Diagnose in the next few weeks, who on your side approves work each week?"
4. "What happens to your number if nothing changes this quarter?"
5. "Is there an incumbent agency we'd work alongside or replace?"

Give a **straight answer on fit**, one of three:

- **Fit:** "Within three business days you'll get the Category Plan: how, who, what it costs and the
  first eight weeks. If it looks right, we start Diagnose as soon as paperwork is done."
- **Not yet:** "Honestly, we're not the right team for this yet, because [budget / timing / scope].
  Here's what I'd do instead: [one of the three moves]."
- **Committee:** "The Category Plan is written to be forwarded. Who should it go to?"

Close on the deck's last line (P.54): *"Control the narrative. Start paving."*

---

## After the call

- **Within three business days:** send the one-page Category Plan (how we'd run it / who'd run it,
  with a named lead per workstream / what it costs / first eight weeks), plus the Snapshot again.
- **One follow-up only.** The page promises no drip sequence.
- **Log it in the CRM:** category, starting pillar, budget band, case shown, fit decision, and the
  UTM source (Calendly receives it from the page).
